# Terrain Decides What Calls Are Possible

## Core rule

You fight the terrain before you fight the enemy.

The same roster can look strong or terrible depending on walls, bridges, chokes, ridges, corners, gates, and retreat paths.

## Terrain questions

Before committing, ask:

- Where can enemy damage land from?
- Where can our damage land from?
- Where can tanks stop the enemy before damage hits?
- Where can healers safely cover danger?
- Where does the zerg go after the engage?
- If we retreat, do we create a clump?
- If we push, are enemy cooldowns spent?

## Terrain tools

Tools often act as terrain extensions:

- firewall extends a wall or lane denial
- wind wall changes a path
- inertia/ring/root turns open ground into a temporary choke
- knockbacks can deny an entry or ruin allied damage if used badly
- Locus/cleanse/speed can turn a dangerous lane into a crossing lane
- tank body position can make a corner safe or unsafe

## Common failure pattern

Players stand where the terrain looks safe but actually creates a perfect bomb:

- hugging a wall
- stacking on a door
- clumping behind a bridge
- walking into a dead end
- all taking the same side of a rock/lake
- backing into the choke instead of spreading through it

## Training prompt

Two approaches lead to the same objective. The short route takes the group through a tight corner. The lead can see the entrance quickly, but the tail can be trapped behind the bend. The longer route has room to spread and may keep more allies in reach of each other, but the group arrives later and needs cover at a different junction.

For each route, draw three lines:

1. enemy engage path
2. friendly damage path
3. friendly exit or next movement path

Mark where each role can first contribute. Can the lead act while the tail is still behind the bend? Where can both groups receive help if defenders hold their ground or force the lead back? Identify a reachable recovery point and the first enemy position that could cut it off. State what the scout still needs to check and whether the route leaves time to do the objective's job after arriving.

Now reveal a fresh report: the enemy has moved from the entrance to the junction on the longer route. That route may still have room to spread, but its recovery point is threatened. Does the shorter route now have a covered approach and enough room for the tail to pass, or is it still too compressed? Revisit the choice. A brief hold or a covered exit may be better than either approach if neither can accomplish the objective in time.

Use the [bridge exercise](chokes-bridges-gates.md) to practise covering the tail once the route is chosen.

## When the exit closes

Your lead reaches a gate as a scout reports enemies taking the far exit. Hold if the near bank still gives cover and the objective allows time. Rotate only if the alternate route can carry the tail. Withdraw through the covered approach while it still works if neither option is available.
