# Threat + Location + Action Callouts

## Core rule

A useful callout tells people what the threat is, where it is, and what action matters.

Bad callout:

> "Brim!"

Better callout:

> "Brim on west wall, spread off the wall."

## Callout formula

> **Threat + location + action**

Examples:

| Weak callout | Stronger callout |
|---|---|
| "Bomb!" | "Bomb squad north side, tanks stop north lane." |
| "Lightcaller!" | "Lightcaller on east door, get off east clump." |
| "They're coming!" | "Enemy turning through south choke, prepare defensives." |
| "Caller deep!" | "Enemy caller deep southwest, punish if safe." |
| "We killed a lot!" | "Enemy west front is down supports; push west now." |

## What to avoid

- item names with no direction
- panic screaming over the caller
- repeating information after the caller has acted on it
- hype claims unsupported by kill feed or vision
- telling the caller what to do unless you are assigned to that role

## Scout / secondary caller note

Report, then stop. The caller needs clean information, not a second person fighting for control of the zerg.

Good:

> "Bomb squad mounted north, five players, moving east toward our backline."

Bad:

> "North north north go north no no back back back!"

## Worked example: a flank report becomes a decision

**Situation:** the main group is holding a gate. A scout sees a small mounted group circling toward the east exit, but cannot see whether its damage is ready.

**Scout:** “Small mounted group east, moving toward our exit; cooldowns unknown.” The scout reports observed movement and uncertainty, then leaves space for the caller.

**Caller:** “Hold the gate. East stopper watch the exit; healers keep the tail in reach. No chase.” This preserves the main job while assigning the new threat.

**Update:** if the group turns away, the scout reports that change. If it dismounts into the lane, the caller can commit cover or rotate before the exit closes. Neither response follows from a style label alone.

**Review:** pause at the first report. Did the caller have enough location and certainty information to assign a response? Check whether the roster moved before the follow-up report, not just whether the eventual fight was won.

## Training prompt

During comms review, mark each callout as:

- useful information
- missing location
- missing action
- repeated/noisy
- over-calling
- morale/hype only
