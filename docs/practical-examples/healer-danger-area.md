# Healers Heal the Danger Area

[Jump to the moving-triage diagram](#moving-triage-diagram){ .tactical-jump }

## Situation

A choke, door, frontline, or caught group is about to take damage.

Healers can be alive and still fail the fight if they are not in range of the danger area.

## Correct response

Healers should identify where damage is actually landing and where the zerg needs to remain functional.

The healing target is often:

- the door/choke being crossed
- the tank line holding the enemy
- the last players through danger
- the group inside a defensive layer
- the players enabling the caller's movement call

## Common failure

Healers stand safely far away and heal the backline while the players making the call possible die unsupported.

<a id="holy-and-nature-together"></a>

## Choose healing by the assigned function

A healing package needs coverage appropriate to the threat: immediate recovery, sustained healing, targeted saves, or route support. Allocate these functions from the selected kits; they are not exclusive to Holy or Nature, and both trees are not mandatory.

## Decide which save is reachable

A low ally returns through the near gate. Healing from your current lane preserves coverage for the crossing. A second ally beyond the far wall may require a different healer or a retreat call. Report the unreachable player before leaving the rest of the group without coverage.

## Review questions

1. Where was the danger area?
2. Could the assigned recovery healer reach it?
3. Did sustained or zone healing support useful space?
4. Did defensive support and healing overlap?
5. Did players stand in the healing area when they should?

## Drill

Your group is withdrawing through a gate. An injured ally is returning along the covered side. The tail is still on the approach, where an enemy group may reach it next. Pause before the injured ally crosses. Who is threatened now, and where will both groups be when your chosen heal takes effect?

- **Targeted heal.** Check whether you can select and reach the intended ally from a usable position. Consider when the heal arrives and where you can go after casting.
- **Area heal.** Place it where the intended players can receive it as they move. A zone that would make them reverse into the threatened lane may cost more than it saves.
- **Other recovery effects.** For a travelling, delayed or sustained effect, check how the selected ability reaches its targets and when it can help. Use the effect you actually have equipped.

??? question "Would you spend the major recovery tool now?"

    If a smaller heal can keep the returning ally alive through the next threat, it may leave group recovery available for the tail. If the smaller heal would arrive too late or provide too little, a major save can be justified. Check who could cover the tail afterward. If neither save is reachable without losing coverage, report the gap and look for a closer recovery point or help from another healer.

Resume the clip and pause as healing takes effect. Check who received it, who could keep moving on the intended route, and what recovery remained for the tail. If the enemy changes direction, reconsider the placement even if nobody's health has changed.
