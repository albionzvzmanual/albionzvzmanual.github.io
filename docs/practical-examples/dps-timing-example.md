# Work Backward from the Assigned Impact

## Situation

A clump appears or a tank locks enemies, and the caller counts down to a synchronized impact. This example is about that assigned burst window; independent picks and sustained pressure are evaluated separately.

The important moment is not when the DPS player starts casting. The important moment is when damage lands.

## Correct response

DPS should already be positioned and ready before the final call.

For a hit that needs a lock and offensive utility, agree where both effects will overlap with damage:

1. clump or lock begins
2. the assigned offensive effect is active when the damage it enables lands
3. damage lands during the control window
4. players follow their next assignment: pressure, cover, or recover

## Common failure

DPS starts a delayed cast at zero. By the time damage lands, enemies have moved, cleansed, healed, or used defensive layers.

## What good DPS watches

- where enemies will be when damage lands
- whether support utility is early enough
- whether the clump is real
- whether enemies are leaving defensive zones
- whether a smaller real clump is better than waiting for a perfect one

## Review questions

1. When did the clump become visible?
2. When did support utility land?
3. When did damage land?
4. Did enemy defensives respond before the damage?
5. Was the DPS in range before the window?

## Drill

Pause before the count ends. Mark the agreed impact, when the target became visible and reachable, when each player was ready to cast, and which effects actually reached the target. Mark when protection or recovery was active. Use another player's recording if one view cannot show the whole exchange.

| What the recording shows | What to review |
| --- | --- |
| The target was available, but you were still moving when you needed to start the cast. | **Late preparation.** Review the approach and the cue that should have started it. |
| You were ready in time, but the effect arrived after the agreed window. | **Late impact.** Compare cast start, travel and arrival. Check whether a delay or interruption also contributed. |
| Allied effects arrived on the count but hit different groups. | **Target mismatch.** Check what was called, what each player could see and whether the effects overlapped. |
| The assigned target was already protected at the agreed impact and your effect landed on time. | **Existing protection.** Review the target choice or how the group intended to answer that protection. |
| Your effect reached the right target, but the expected second hit or utility could not arrive. | **Missing allied follow-through.** Find out what prevented the other contribution before coaching the person whose effect landed. |

Several causes can occur in one hit. If nobody could reach the target in time, review the call or setup before blaming an individual's cast.
