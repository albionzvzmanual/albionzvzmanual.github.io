# Kite as a Unit

## Situation

The caller tells the zerg to kite or back up.

Some players move correctly, some abandon the group, and others stay too long.

## Correct response

Move far enough to reduce the enemy's pressure while keeping the withdrawing group connected. Stay in range to turn only if allies have the tools, coverage and reason to do so.

A good kite:

- reduces enemy damage value
- keeps healers/supports connected
- protects the last players through danger
- preserves the option to turn when a supported opportunity exists
- keeps a usable route for the tail even if the group needs to leave completely

## Role response

| Role | During kite |
|---|---|
| Assigned rear cover | Protect the pursuit lane while your tools and recovery route remain usable. Armor alone does not determine who stays. |
| Healers | keep range on endangered players |
| Supports | cover the enemy pressure path |
| Ranged DPS | Cover a reachable lane if assigned. Stay in range for a turn only when the plan and coverage support it. |
| Safe players | Follow the exit route, avoid bunching where the enemy can hit several allies, and stay within reach of anyone you are assigned to help. |

## Common failure

Players move away without checking who still needs cover. That can leave the tail exposed. If the caller has planned a turn, it can also leave the group too far apart to act together.

## Review questions

1. Did the group reach a covered exit or preserve a supported turn if the situation allowed one?
2. Were healers/supports still connected?
3. Who was assigned to cover the last players, and could they do it before leaving?
4. Where did a player lose the reach needed for their job?
5. Which players stayed too long?

## Drill

Pause after the first move away. Ask:

> Can the tail and the previous cover player reach help? Is there a supported reason to turn, or should the group finish leaving?

## Hand off the tail

Before the rear cover leaves, check that the replacement can see and reach the pursuit lane, has an appropriate effect ready, and can leave afterward. Identify where the tail and the previous cover player can receive help as they move. A player can be on the far side of the gate and still be outside useful recovery.

If the replacement cannot act or the recovery point is unreachable, look for a closer position that both cover players can reach, or continue along a covered exit if the group has to leave. A withdrawal succeeds when the group gets the people it can cover out of the threat and reaches a position where it can recover. A safe disengagement is a complete result, even when there is no counterattack.
