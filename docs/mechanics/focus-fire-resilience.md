# Focus Fire Protection and resilience penetration

When several opponents attack one player, Focus Fire Protection reduces the damage that player takes from their attacks. The protection grows with attacker count. Adding another attacker can still help secure a kill, but a caller cannot estimate the group's damage by multiplying one player's hit by the number of attackers.

## Decide what concentrating damage would accomplish

At a doorway, an enemy tank is inside your line while the enemy damage group is still behind cover. If the tank is isolated, vulnerable, and blocking your exit, focused damage may clear a route. Check the tank's visible defenses, healing access and the cost of committing your main damage. If the tank can be contained with a stop, saving that damage for the next wave may protect the doorway longer.

The same distinction matters in review. A durable target surviving several hits does not identify the cause. Check how many attackers actually connected, what healing or shields landed, and whether the hits arrived together. A delayed second wave can give defenders time to recover.

## Protection and penetration

**Protection** is a defensive effect on the target. **Resilience Penetration** concerns how much of that particular protection an attack can bypass. Armor reduction acts on a different defense. Treat these as separate claims when reading an old build or comparing weapons.

Dragonfire set Resilience Penetration to zero for a Season 34 trial. The Season 34 announcement confirmed its scope. Season 35 began on 21 November. Its start announcement describes changes due with Patch 5 and supplies no current penetration rule. Weapon rankings based on older penetration values therefore remain unresolved for Season 35.

??? question "Would splitting damage always improve the result?"

    Splitting may spread pressure and make an opponent use several saves. It may also prevent your group from finishing a vulnerable target in a brief window. Choose a result you can observe, such as forcing a defender off the exit, and judge the call against that result.
