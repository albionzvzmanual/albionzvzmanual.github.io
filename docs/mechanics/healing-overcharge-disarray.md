# Healing sickness, overcharge and disarray

A party reaches a gate with injured frontliners, missing heal coverage on the tail and reinforcements waiting to enter the region. Three different systems matter here. Each answers a different question.

## Healing sickness changes the value of overlapping heals

Receiving healing from multiple healers can reduce the healing a target receives. That overlap is called **healing sickness**. A group cannot assume a second healer on the same target will double the recovery. The selected abilities matter. For example, a change to Protection of Nature added a direct heal that triggered healing sickness.

At the gate, one healer can cover the first wave while another keeps the crossing group in reach. Agree when the second healer should help with an endangered frontliner and who covers the tail during that handoff. An urgent save can justify overlap. Check the next incoming damage, whether the primary healer can reach, and who would be left uncovered. Holy and Nature kits can both contribute to these assignments.

## Overcharge trades equipment risk for temporary power

Overcharge temporarily increases an item's power and carries a risk of item loss when the charge ends. Paths to Glory introduced an option to extend the duration with a second overcharge at greater breakage risk. Review the live charge prompt and your group's replacement arrangements before deciding. A shortened fight or a victory does not eliminate the expiry risk.

Ask what the extra power would change in the coming objective window. If the party is waiting outside the region, connected numbers and arrival time may matter more than immediate activation.

## Disarray changes the effect of group size

Disarray is a size-based group debuff. Read the group's displayed status alongside local conditions. Regional headcount, people present at this doorway and players able to contribute to this exchange can all differ. Earlier queue rules even counted queued reinforcements toward the receiving region's disarray headcount. Verify the live mode and rules before using roster arithmetic to predict a penalty.

??? question "A large party arrives, yet the tail keeps dying. What would you review first?"

    Mark where each healer could reach, which targets received overlapping heals and when reinforcements actually crossed. Compare the group's status and charge state with those observations. A debuff icon alone cannot tell you whether the tail received any healing.
